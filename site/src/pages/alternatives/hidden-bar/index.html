A Hidden Bar alternative for notched MacBooks

Hidden Bar is a free, simple way to hide menu bar icons behind an arrow. On a MacBook with a notch, the icons it reveals can land behind the notch too. Coat Check takes a different approach.

Updated October 19, 2026

The short version

Hidden Bar adds an arrow to your menu bar. ⌘-drag icons to its left and click the arrow to hide or show them. It’s free and MIT licensed, and simple to set up.

The catch on a notched MacBook: when Hidden Bar reveals icons, they still have to fit to the right of the notch. If there isn’t room, they stay out of sight. Coat Check lists every icon in a menu under its hanger instead, so the notch can’t cover them, and opens any of them from there.

On macOS 27

macOS 27 stopped allowing menu bar items to stretch very wide, which is how Hidden Bar used to push icons away. Version 1.11.1 from GitHub works around it, but the Mac App Store version can’t hide icons on macOS 27. If you use Hidden Bar on 27, install it from GitHub.

What works differently

  • Hidden Bar hides; Coat Check reaches. One clears space in your menu bar, the other gets you to icons that are already out of sight.
  • Coat Check shows each app’s menu. Choose an item right from the list.
  • Both are free and MIT licensed.

Using both

Coat Check doesn’t move or hide anything unless you turn on its divider, so it can run alongside Hidden Bar. Use Hidden Bar to arrange and hide icons, and Coat Check to open any icon from one list.
